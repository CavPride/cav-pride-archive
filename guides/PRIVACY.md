# Privacy: footage of minors

This archive shows children. Decide on purpose who can see it. Here is what each setting does and does not do.

## The honest summary

- **Unlisted is not private.** An unlisted video is hidden from search and from your channel, but anyone who has the link can watch it, forward it or download it with free tools.
- **This website is not private either.** On a free GitHub account the site must be public. Its address is guessable from your username and repository name, and `data/games.json` (which lists every video ID) is readable by anyone who finds the site. The site asks search engines not to list it (a `noindex` tag), but that is a request, not a lock.
- **A password on this site would not help.** A password check written in the page itself can be read and skipped by anyone.

So the real protection is who you give the address to.

## YouTube visibility, side by side

| | Public | Unlisted | Private |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Who can watch | Anyone, and it shows in search | Anyone with the link, no account needed | Only Google accounts you invite, signed in, up to 50 |
| In search and on your channel | Yes | No | No |
| Can play on this website | Yes | **Yes** | **No** |
| Easy for many families | Yes | **Yes** | No: each family needs a Google account and you add each address |
| Link forwarding | Anyone | Works for anyone | Does not work for people you did not invite |

Private is the only setting that truly limits viewers, but it cannot be embedded, so it does not work with this website. It also caps at 50 accounts per video, which is tight if several families each have two parents plus grandparents.

## Recommended setup (free, practical)

1. Upload every game as **Unlisted**.
2. Turn **Allow embedding** on and **Comments** off.
3. Share the **website address** with the team families by text message or a group chat. Do not post it on social media or the school's public pages.
4. Ask families not to repost the link.
5. Keep titles and descriptions plain: "Game 1 vs. Opponent". Skip players' full names, jersey numbers tied to names, addresses and phone numbers.
6. Use two-step verification on the Google account that owns the channel. Everything hangs off it.
7. If a family asks you to remove their child, you can set a video to Private or delete the entry from `games.json`.

This balances ease and privacy for a group that is trusting but not locked down. If any family wants stricter control, use **Private** for those videos and invite specific Google accounts in YouTube Studio. Those videos will not play on the website, and they are watched on YouTube itself.

## Things to decide before you share

- **Other families' children** appear in your footage. Ask the other parents before you share the address, and tell them it is link-based, not locked.
- **The logo.** The shield belongs to the school. A family archive shared among team families is a different matter from a public site, and if you ever make it wider, ask the athletic department. The footer says the site is not an official school website.
- **Music and copyrighted audio** in a game video (bands, speakers) can trigger automatic claims on YouTube, which may mute or block a video.
- **YouTube's "made for kids" question** is asked for every upload. Answer for the real audience. It is your call, and it affects comments and some features.

## What I could not verify

YouTube and GitHub change their rules. The details above, including the 50-account limit and public-only free Pages, were checked against current sources at the time of writing. Confirm them on YouTube's and GitHub's own help pages before relying on them.
